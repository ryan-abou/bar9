Subject: Why the piano is named after one word
Preview text: Around 1700, a keyboard finally learned to play soft and loud.

---

This is the first Bar 9. Thanks for being here from the start.

Before the piano, the keyboard most people played was the harpsichord. Press a
harpsichord key and a small quill plucks the string. It doesn't matter much how
hard you press. The pluck comes out about the same, so every note does too.

Around 1700 in Florence, Bartolomeo Cristofori, who looked after the Medici
family's instruments, built a keyboard where each key threw a small hammer at the
string instead. Press lightly and you get a soft note. Press hard and it's loud.
His trick for getting the hammer back out of the way, the escapement, is still
inside every piano.

In 1711 a writer called it a "gravicembalo col piano e forte": a harpsichord with
soft and loud. That got shortened to pianoforte, and then just piano.
